Reporting a 4-H or FFA Project on Schedule 1, Line 8z (With a Sample Statement)

If the project isn't a trade or business, its net income goes on one line: Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 8z, “Other income.” Here is how that line and the attached statement fit together.

The line

IRS Publication 225 says to report a 4-H or FFA project's net income as “Other income” on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 8z. The draft 2026 Schedule 1 still has line 8z, labeled “Other income. List type and amount.” Older documents, like the National 4-H fact sheet, say “line 21 of Form 1040”; that was the line before Schedule 1 existed.

The 2026 Schedule 1 was a draft (not for filing) on November 23, 2026. Line numbers can change. Check the final form.

The statement

Pub. 225 says to attach a statement showing gross income and expenses if necessary. It helps most when a 1099-MISC reports the full auction payment and the return shows the smaller net amount. A simple statement lists:

  • the child's name and the tax year,
  • each type of project income (sale, add-ons, prizes),
  • each category of expense,
  • the net amount, and the amount entered on line 8z,
  • any 1099 received and its amount.

When line 8z may not be the right place

If the project is regular, recurring, and run for profit, or the child has other farming, the Ohio State University Extension bulletin describes Schedule F instead, with self-employment tax. That is a preparer decision.

Common questions

Where do I report 4-H or FFA project income?

IRS Publication 225 says to report the net income as Other income on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 8z, and to attach a statement showing the gross income and expenses if necessary.

What do I write on line 8z?

Line 8z asks you to list the type and amount of other income. A short description such as 4-H/FFA project net income, with the net amount, is the usual approach. Confirm with your preparer or software.

Do I need to attach a statement?

Pub. 225 says to attach one if necessary. It is useful when a 1099-MISC shows the gross amount and the return shows the smaller net amount. How to include it with an e-filed return depends on the software.
