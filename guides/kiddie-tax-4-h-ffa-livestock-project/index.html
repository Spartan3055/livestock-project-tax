The Kiddie Tax and 4-H / FFA Livestock Projects: When $2,700 Matters

Most livestock projects never get close. But a big sale with strong add-ons can push a child's unearned income past $2,700, and that is where Form 8615 comes in.

The rule

The draft 2026 Instructions for Form 8615 say that for children under 18 and certain older children, unearned income over $2,700 is taxed at the parent's rate if that rate is higher than the child's. Form 8615 must be filed for a child who:

  1. had more than $2,700 of unearned income,
  2. is required to file a tax return,
  3. was under 18 at the end of 2026, or was 18 (or a full-time student 19 to 23) and didn't have earned income of more than half of their own support,
  4. had at least one living parent at the end of 2026, and
  5. doesn't file a joint return.

Draft, not for filing. The $2,700 equals twice the $1,350 base amount in Rev. Proc. 2025-32, section 3.02. Check the final 2026 Form 8615 instructions.

Why “earned or unearned” matters so much here

The kiddie tax only reaches unearned income. The Ohio State University Extension tax bulletin explains that project income reported as other income is “most likely” not earned income, while a project treated as a business on Schedule F produces earned income instead. The same bulletin notes the choice also affects things like self-employment tax and whether the child can contribute to an IRA. This is a decision to make with a preparer, not one a calculator should make.

Example

Example numbers only. A grand champion steer sells for $9,000 with $1,500 in add-ons. The steer cost $1,800 and feed, vet, and fees came to $2,400. Net project income is $6,300. If treated as unearned, that is well over $2,700, so Form 8615 may apply. If treated as a business, it would be earned income with self-employment tax instead. Either way, this is a return for a preparer.

Common questions

What is the kiddie tax threshold for 2026?

The draft 2026 Instructions for Form 8615 say Form 8615 is used when a child's unearned income is more than $2,700 and the other conditions are met. The draft was not final when this page was written.

Does the kiddie tax apply to 4-H steer money?

Only if the project income is unearned income and the child's unearned income is over $2,700, along with the age and support conditions. Whether project income is unearned is a question for a tax preparer.

Which children does Form 8615 cover?

Per the draft 2026 instructions: children under 18 at the end of 2026, children who were 18 and whose earned income was not more than half their support, and full-time students 19 to 23 whose earned income was not more than half their support, who must file a return and have at least one living parent.
