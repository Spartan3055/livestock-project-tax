Does My Child Need to File a Tax Return After an FFA Steer Sale? (2026 Rules)

Educational only. Not tax advice.

Tax rules depend on each family's facts, and the earned-versus-unearned question for project income is not settled for every case. Check your numbers with a tax preparer or the IRS before you file.

It usually comes down to one number: the child's net project income compared with the 2026 dependent lines. Here is the test, with examples.

The 2026 test for a dependent child

Rev. Proc. 2025-32 sets the 2026 standard deduction for someone who can be claimed as a dependent at the greater of $1,350 or earned income plus $450, and the basic single standard deduction at $16,100. IRS Publication 501 (Table 2) turns those figures into a filing test. Using the 2026 figures, a single dependent under 65 and not blind must file if any of these apply:

  1. Unearned income was more than $1,350.
  2. Earned income was more than $16,100.
  3. Gross income was more than the larger of $1,350, or earned income (up to $15,650) plus $450.

The 2026 edition of Pub. 501 was not yet published on November 23, 2026. The test above uses the 2025 table's structure with the 2026 dollar figures from Rev. Proc. 2025-32. Confirm against the final 2026 Pub. 501.

Other rules can require a return too, such as net self-employment earnings of $400 or more (Pub. 501).

Which line does a steer sale fall under?

If the project is educational and not a trade or business, the net income goes on Schedule 1, line 8z as other income (Pub. 225), and Ohio State University Extension says it is “most likely” not earned income. In that case, it counts toward the $1,350 unearned-income line. If the project is treated as a business on Schedule F, it becomes earned income instead. That choice changes the answer, so it is the first question for a preparer.

Common questions

What is the 2026 filing threshold for a dependent child?

For a single dependent under 65 and not blind, using 2026 figures from Rev. Proc. 2025-32 applied to the Pub. 501 Table 2 test: a return is required if unearned income is more than $1,350, earned income is more than $16,100, or gross income is more than the larger of $1,350 or earned income (up to $15,650) plus $450.

Is steer sale money earned or unearned income?

It depends on the facts. Ohio State University Extension says project income reported as other income is most likely not earned income, but it is a question to confirm with a tax preparer, especially if the project could be a trade or business.

Should my child file even if it's not required?

Sometimes filing is simpler, for example when a 1099-MISC was issued. A tax preparer can tell you whether it helps in your case.
