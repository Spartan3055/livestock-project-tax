Can You Subtract Feed and Vet Costs From a 4-H or FFA Project Sale?

Yes, in the sense that matters: Pub. 225 says to report a 4-H or FFA project's net income, which is income minus project expenses. Good records are what make that number hold up.

What the IRS says

Publication 225 says net income from sales or prizes related to the project may be subject to income tax, to report the net income as other income on Schedule 1, line 8z, and to attach a statement showing the gross income and expenses if necessary. The National 4-H Headquarters fact sheet and the Ohio State University Extension tax bulletin describe it the same way.

Two things to watch

  • Who paid. If parents paid for feed and the child kept the whole check, raise that with a preparer.
  • A loss. If expenses beat the sale, line 8z gets $0. Whether a loss is usable depends on whether the project is a trade or business, which is a preparer question.

Common questions

Can I subtract feed costs from my child's 4-H project income?

IRS Publication 225 says to report the net income from a 4-H or FFA project, which means gross income minus project expenses, and to attach a statement showing gross income and expenses if necessary.

What if my child's project lost money?

If expenses are larger than income, there is no net income to report on line 8z. Whether a loss can be used at all depends on whether the project is a trade or business. Ask a tax preparer.

Who has to pay the expenses?

The expenses should be the project's expenses. If a parent paid for feed and the child did not reimburse, ask a preparer how that affects the calculation.
