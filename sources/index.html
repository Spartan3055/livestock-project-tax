Documents

  • Rev. Proc. 2025-32 (2026 inflation adjustments), IRSfinal
    Section 3.14: 2026 dependent standard deduction is the greater of $1,350 or earned income plus $450; single standard deduction $16,100. Section 3.02: kiddie-tax base amount $1,350.
  • Publication 225, Farmer's Tax Guide (for 2025 returns), IRSfinal
    Chapter 12, “4-H Club or FFA project”: report net income as other income on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 8z; attach a statement of gross income and expenses if necessary; may not be subject to SE tax if primarily educational and not for profit. Also SE tax rate 15.3% on 92.35% of net earnings. The 2026 edition was not yet published.
  • Publication 501 (2025), Dependents, Standard Deduction, and Filing Information, IRSfinal
    Table 2, filing requirements for dependents (2025 version). Used for the structure of the test only; the 2026 dollar figures on this site come from Rev. Proc. 2025-32 because the 2026 Pub. 501 was not yet published. Also: net earnings from self-employment of at least $400 require a return.
  • Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC (Rev. December 2026), IRSfinal
    For tax years beginning after 2025, the minimum reporting threshold increased to $2,000 (may be adjusted for inflation starting 2027). Box 3, Other income: $2,000 or more.
  • 2026 Instructions for Form 8615 (draft, dated June 11, 2026), IRSDRAFT form
    Form 8615 is required for a child with more than $2,700 of unearned income who must file and meets the age and support conditions. Draft, not for filing.
  • 2026 Schedule 1 (Form 1040) (draft), IRSDRAFT form
    Line 8z, “Other income. List type and amount.” Draft, not for filing.
  • National 4-H Headquarters fact sheet, “Livestock Sales and Prize Winnings Related to 4-H Programs” (hosted by Ohio State University Extension, Shelby County)older document
    Net income is not subject to self-employment tax if the project is primarily educational and not for profit. Older document: it cites the earlier $600 1099 threshold and “line 21 of Form 1040,” which is now Schedule 1, line 8z.
  • Ohio State University Extension Tax Bulletin, “Sale of 4-H or FFA Projects Has Tax Implications” (January 2014)older document
    Explains the two reporting alternatives (other income vs. Schedule F) and says project net income “most likely” is not earned income for the dependent standard deduction. Uses 2014 dollar figures; only the reasoning is used here.
  • Michigan Farm Bureau, “Livestock Auction Tax Reporting Requirements” FAQ (July 2026)
    Some fairs now collect W-9s; the $2,000 threshold counts all qualifying payments in the calendar year, not per auction; a 1099-MISC does not automatically mean tax is owed.
